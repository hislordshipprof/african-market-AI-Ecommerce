# Market sites review: African, Caribbean and independent grocery storefronts

Date: 2026-10-30. Method: live visits in the built-in browser at desktop (~800px pane) and mobile (375x812),
computed styles read with JS (colors ranked by frequency, font families). No logins, no cart actions,
non-essential cookies declined where a Decline control existed. Hexes below are converted from computed `rgb()`.

## African / ethnic players

### Danlex Wholesale Foods (danlexwholesalefoods.com), Calgary, Canada
- **Impression:** The most modern of the African set. Next.js build, a full-bleed promo carousel (palm-oil banner),
  a purple marquee ticker of product names in italic serif ("Egusi · Garden Eggs · Gari · Suya Spice"),
  scroll-reveal fades, a green city ticker, and a pill-shaped WhatsApp FAB.
- **Palette:** ink plum `#261B32` (text), purple `#5B2D8E`, green `#206F41` / `#1B6B3A` (primary CTA), orange `#E88B2D`, gold `#E8AB3A`.
- **Fonts:** Fraunces (display serif, 700) + DM Sans. A good pairing.
- **Cards:** white cards with soft radius (12px), a small uppercase category eyebrow ("TUBERS"), serif product name, "From $x" price, round cart icon.
- **Photography:** packshots on white, some cut out. The hero banners are loud, flyer-style graphics with heavy text.
- **Trust:** reviews section, customer stars, city list, WhatsApp.
- **Mobile:** has an app-like bottom tab bar (Home/Shop/Cart/Account/More). Good. But the hero is a letterboxed banner on black, and scroll reveals leave large blank areas until they fire.
- **Premium vs cheap:** the type and structure feel premium. The flyer banners and the purple-to-green gradient CTA band feel cheap and off-brand.

### My Sasun (mysasun.com), Texas (Celina/Houston)
- **Impression:** A busy Shopify theme. A maroon top bar, a mustard-yellow header, a QR "get the app" popup,
  an app-install banner, a rewards widget, a referral modal and a cookie sheet, all at once. On mobile the first screen is about 80% overlays.
- **Palette:** maroon `#70062C`, mustard `#EDBE5E`, cream `#F4F2E9`, near-black `#1D1D1D`.
- **Fonts:** Cabin only (headings at weight 400, so the hierarchy is weak).
- **Cards:** tall white cards, a maroon "SAVE UP TO $12" badge, own-label packaging (Sasun Garri Ijebu).
- **Photography:** a real cooked jollof hero (good), plus packshots.
- **Trust:** a four-icon service strip (shipping / delivery radius / 2-hour pickup / 48-hour complaints), store selector ("My store: Dallas, closing at 6pm"), phone/text.
- **Premium vs cheap:** the maroon/cream base and the service strip are strong. The popup stacking and a CTA band with no hero image loaded make it feel cheap.

### Abart Foods (abartfoods.com), Beltsville, MD
- **Impression:** WordPress/WooCommerce plus Elementor. A banner with a stock "chef" photo and grocery cut-outs, then a two-column "International Groceries Delivered To Your Door" block.
- **Palette:** leaf green `#8BC34A`, orange `#E65C00`, off-white `#F8F6F3`, grey text `#333333`.
- **Fonts:** Open Sans + Merriweather. Some headings fall back to a Times-like serif, which reads as broken.
- **Cards:** horizontal-scroll rails per category. Many cards show an **"ABARTFOODS IMAGE COMING SOON"** placeholder, and some have empty price skeletons. Round orange "+" add button.
- **Mobile:** stacked and usable, but tiny banner text, centered serif headings and a large green hamburger button.
- **Premium vs cheap:** dated. Missing product photos are the single biggest credibility hit seen in this review.

### Afelabu (afelabu.com), Houston, TX
- **Impression:** A clean Shopify theme with rounded geometry. Strong merchandising: a "One Box, The Whole Family Table" Thanksgiving hamper ($59.99 / $149.75, order by Nov 19), monthly "Student Packs", and Shop daily / Order in bulk / Ship anywhere tiles.
- **Palette:** forest green `#06623B` (dominant), bright yellow `#FED507` (buttons), `#121212` text. The Nigerian-flag green is clear.
- **Fonts:** Pally (rounded geometric) for everything.
- **Cards:** large rounded cards with real bagged-product photos (beans, Ijebu garri, palm oil).
- **Mobile:** the hero image did not load at first, leaving a blank space above "Shop Now". Category tiles had no images.
- **Premium vs cheap:** friendly and tidy. The flag-color scheme and the empty image slots pull it down.

### ETNOWE (etnowe.com), multi-cuisine delivery app (6 states, 30+ stores)
- **Impression:** The most "startup-polished" site. A warm cream canvas, teal accents, a cuisine marquee (Mexican · Indian · Korean · Ethiopian...), food cards with rounded photos, and "Kitchens open near you" chips.
- **Palette:** teal `#0A5F66` / `#08757F`, mint `#7FD4C1`, cream `#FCF9F6`, sand `#F3EAE0`, ink `#17120E`, taupe `#8A8175`.
- **Fonts:** Hanken Grotesk (light 300 headings) + DM Mono for small uppercase eyebrows.
- **Photography:** a hero of a smiling woman in an Ankara headwrap that looks AI-generated, and styled restaurant-quality plates (jollof with chicken and plantain, bibimbap).
- **Premium vs cheap:** the palette and type are premium. The stock or AI "headwrap woman" hero is the clichéd move. Light 300 headings over a pale gradient have weak contrast.

### African Dishout / Dishout (africandishout.com), NY/NJ/MA
- **Impression:** A bold poster style. A forest-green field with huge condensed caps in yellow, a cut-out jollof plate and grocery bag, city pills.
- **Palette:** forest `#00492C`, yellow `#FFBA16`, cream `#FFFCDE`. Links keep the default blue `#0000EE`, which looks unfinished.
- **Fonts:** obviously-narrow (condensed 800) + Figtree, with Arial fallback in places.
- **Premium vs cheap:** confident and memorable, but it reads as a delivery app ad, not a grocer. It also leans on the yellow we are avoiding.

### OjaExpress (ojaexpress.com), Chicago, multi-ethnic marketplace
- **Impression:** Broken. The hero, logo, category and store images all failed and show alt text ("Family Shopping", "Item 1"). Peach sections and a teal CTA.
- **Palette:** teal `#00B89F`, orange `#FF7C00`, peach `#FFD5AF`. **Fonts:** Roboto.
- **Note:** shows stores as cards with cuisine tags (Caribbean / African / Hispanic), which is a useful pattern for our Store directory.

### Not reviewable
- **Ethnic District** (ethnicdistrict.com, Atlanta): the domain now redirects to an Indonesian gambling site (lapsed or hijacked). No other storefront URL found.
- **Afayi** (afayi.com, DFW): the domain is parked for sale on GoDaddy. It appears to live only in directories.
- **Motherland Grocery** (Chicago): TLS certificate error, so the page did not load.

## Non-African benchmarks

### Good Eggs (goodeggs.com), SF Bay Area
- **Palette:** deep green `#306043` (pill CTAs, radius 23-26px), teal `#6AB5B1`, tomato `#EA4135` (sale), cream `#F9F8F4`.
- **Fonts:** Concrette (soft display serif) + Graphik.
- **Strengths:** a real lifestyle hero (a branded box overflowing with produce), a dense mega-nav, "Shop for Tomorrow in 90039" delivery-promise copy, SAVE % badges, unit price ($/oz) on every card, and quick-add "+" circles on photos.
- **Weak:** three overlays at once (offer, quiz, cookie banner, which has no Decline button).

### Sahadi's (sahadis.com), Brooklyn Middle Eastern grocer, 100+ years old (Shopify)
- **Palette:** near-monochrome, `#1D1D1D` on white, with tiny touches of oxblood `#731718` and tan `#E1C5A4`.
- **Fonts:** sweet-gothic-serif (wide display caps) + Archivo Narrow.
- **Strengths:** the closest analogue to our stores, a heritage independent. The heritage logotype, catering/events/locations in the nav, and **overhead editorial photos of the actual goods** (spices, cinnamon sticks, dried apricots, Turkish coffee in a patterned cup) as category tiles make it feel expensive with no color spend.
- **Weak:** the hero heading breaks mid-word ("GUARANTEE / D"), and an email-capture popup.

### Misfits Market (misfitsmarket.com)
- **Palette:** cream `#FFF9EA`, charcoal `#2D2D2D`, mustard `#F1C34A` (CTA), green `#2E8540`, sage `#B0C471`.
- **Fonts:** a custom grotesk, bold for display.
- **Strengths:** a full-bleed video hero of hands holding produce, a press-logo marquee, and rounded colored tiles with produce photos. Motion is used with purpose. **Weak:** a marketing-site feel, so you have to click "Get Started" before you can shop.

### Mercato (mercato.com), marketplace for independent grocers
- **Palette:** teal `#01796F` / `#01564F`, mint `#E7F4F2`, ink `#25272C`, sand `#D4BA8A`. **Fonts:** Poppins only.
- Generic, safe SaaS look. Every store page looks alike, and the store's own brand is muted. This is the anti-pattern our Storefront concept should beat.

## Gap: where these sites look weak (our opportunity)
1. **Missing or broken imagery is common**: Abart "image coming soon", OjaExpress alt text, blank heroes on Afelabu and Danlex mobile, Sasun's empty band. A default catalog image set from the Master product catalog (consistent packshots on a neutral surface), plus graceful skeletons, would put every store ahead on day one.
2. **Popup overload**: Sasun and Good Eggs stack 3-5 overlays. One quiet, dismissible bar and a cookie banner that has a Decline button is our baseline.
3. **Flyer-style hero banners** (text baked into images, low resolution, many fonts) read as cheap and break on mobile. Use real HTML text over photography, and template banners the store owner fills in.
4. **Weak type hierarchy**: Cabin 400 headings, Times fallbacks, one font everywhere. A disciplined serif + sans pairing (Danlex, Good Eggs, Sahadi's) is the clearest premium signal.
5. **Local trust is under-sold**: only Sasun shows "my store, closing at 6pm" and a delivery radius. None show the owner's story, the store's neighborhood, pickup windows or Swap rules up front. Store-specific trust (owner photo, years open, real shopfront) is our differentiator against app aggregators.
6. **Local names and search**: product names are English-only or brand-only. Showing local names and synonyms (e.g. "Egusi / melon seed") on cards and in search is unclaimed.
7. **Mobile shopping is shallow**: only Danlex has a bottom tab bar. Sticky cart, thumb-reach add buttons and category chips are rare.
8. **Unit price and pack sizes** appear only on Good Eggs. Bulk vs retail sizes (Afelabu) deserve a proper size selector.
9. **Bundles work** (Afelabu Thanksgiving box, student packs), but nobody presents them as a first-class, beautiful module (soup kits, "jollof night", "Sunday stew").
10. **Aggregator sameness** (Mercato, OjaExpress): stores lose their identity. Strong per-store theming (logo, accent color, hero photo) on a shared system is the core opportunity.

## Cultural cues that work
- **Food as hero, shot honestly**: overhead plates of jollof, egusi or pepper soup, raw ingredients in bowls, enamel and calabash. Sahadi's-style editorial flat-lays of the actual goods feel authentic and premium.
- **Pantry vernacular**: real bags of garri, bottles of red palm oil, tins of Milo, bundles of dried fish. Showing the familiar packaging builds recognition ("I know that brand").
- **Marquee of local staples in a serif** (Danlex), and **local-name eyebrows** (TUBERS, SOUPS & STEWS): warm, specific, cheap to build.
- **Textile pattern as accent, not wallpaper**: a thin kente, adire or mud-cloth inspired band or divider, a tinted pattern on empty states, or a pattern on packaging-style labels. Keep it at low contrast and small area.
- **Warm earth neutrals** (cream, sand, clay) as canvas instead of stark white. They echo market stalls and woven baskets.
- **Community and people**: the owner and staff at the counter, real customers (with consent), the actual storefront. Authentic, unlike stock models.
- **Clichéd or to avoid:** AI or stock "smiling woman in headwrap" heroes (ETNOWE), flag-color schemes (green+yellow), map-of-Africa silhouettes, generic "tribal" fonts, drum and mask clip-art, whole-page kente wallpaper, and poster-style yellow caps.

## Color observations
- **Seen across the category:** green dominates (6 of 9 African sites), usually paired with yellow, orange or a flag palette. Maroon/oxblood (Sasun, Sahadi's) and teal (ETNOWE, Mercato, OjaExpress) are the premium outliers. Cream canvases (`#F9F8F4`, `#FCF9F6`, `#FFF9EA`, `#F4F2E9`) appear on every polished site.
- **Constraints:** no yellow or amber (this excludes Afelabu `#FED507`, Dishout `#FFBA16`, Misfits `#F1C34A`, Sasun `#EDBE5E` and gold accents like `#E8AB3A`). No purple/violet gradients (this excludes Danlex's `#5B2D8E` gradient band).
- **Candidate platform palette (store-themable accent on top):**

| Role | Name | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas | Shea cream | `#FBF7F1` | warm off-white, like ETNOWE/Good Eggs but less pink |
| Surface 2 | Sand | `#F1E8DC` | section bands, card wells |
| Ink | Kola ink | `#1C1714` | warm near-black, softer than pure black |
| Muted text | Clay grey | `#6E625A` | taupe secondary text (4.5:1+ on cream) |
| Primary | Palm forest | `#1F5A43` | deep, desaturated green; category-native without flag brightness |
| Primary hover | Deep palm | `#174635` | |
| Accent A | Hibiscus / zobo | `#9E2B3F` | oxblood-red from zobo/sorrel drinks; premium like Sasun/Sahadi's |
| Accent B | Red palm oil / clay | `#C2502E` | terracotta-orange for sale/badges; not amber |
| Accent C | Indigo adire | `#23415C` | Yoruba adire indigo dye; links/info, a calm cool counterweight |
| Accent D | Lagoon teal | `#1D6F6A` | alternate store theme option (Caribbean stores) |
| Success | Leaf | `#3C7D4F` | stock "in stock" chip |
| Border | Raffia | `#E3D7C7` | hairlines on cream |

- **Store themes:** let each store pick one accent from a curated, contrast-checked set (palm forest, hibiscus, indigo adire, lagoon teal, clay, cocoa `#5A3A2A`), applied to buttons, links and the pattern band. Keep the canvas, ink and type fixed so every Storefront stays professional.
- **Motion note:** the motion that worked was a slow marquee (Danlex, ETNOWE), a gentle scroll fade (Danlex, though its delay was too long and left blank areas), and a muted video hero (Misfits). Keep reveals under 300ms, show content immediately on first paint, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
